## Brand Essence

Whalefall should feel personal, raw, nocturnal, coastal, and emotionally heavy without becoming slick or corporate.

Working phrase:

> emotional noise from berkeley, ca

Brand adjectives:

- Handmade
- Noisy
- Intimate
- Salt-dark
- Slightly haunted
- DIY
- Tactile
- Unpolished but intentional
- Authentic
- Intense
- Fun in a rough-room way

Genre language from Drive:

- Alt-rock
- Garage rock
- Indie
- Experimental rock
- Shoegaze

Comparable artist references from Drive:

- Wednesday
- Title Fight
- My Bloody Valentine / MBV
- Black Country, New Road / BCNR

Known/referenced songs:

- `Starboy`
- `The Runner`
- `For You, to Blame`

Avoid:

- Startup polish
- Glossy gradients
- Corporate minimalism
- Generic indie-band templates
- Overproduced festival-poster styling
- Cute ocean/whale literalism unless the band asks for it
- Anything that makes them feel like a generic “sad indie band” instead of a specific Berkeley band with room noise, friends, school-year shows, and process

## Visual Identity

### Logo / Mark Direction

Observed profile image:

- Circular emblem
- Deep navy center
- Warm off-white outer field
- White hand-drawn ring motif that reads like crabs, hands, bones, or small bent figures
- Lo-fi, photocopied, hand-inked energy

Use this as the core visual direction:

- Prefer circular, stamped, photocopied, or scanned-feeling marks.
- Keep linework imperfect.
- Use off-white ink on dark navy, or navy ink on off-white.
- Favor simple one-color marks over detailed illustrations.
- Let edges feel human rather than mechanically perfect.
- Keep room for a future whale-with-X-eyes motif if the band confirms it. A Drive note mentions “Whale with x eyes” as a merch/visual idea.

Do not recreate or redraw the exact Instagram profile mark unless the band provides permission or source files.

## Color Palette

Colors sampled from the public Instagram profile image.

### Core Colors

| Role | Hex | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep Navy | `#253056` | Primary brand color, backgrounds, headers, buttons, borders |
| Almost Black Navy | `#192036` | Deep backgrounds, footer, high-contrast text blocks |
| Warm Bone | `#E9EAE8` | Page background, paper texture, logo contrast |
| Weathered Blue Gray | `#51576B` | Secondary UI, captions, muted borders |
| Fog Gray | `#979CAA` | Subtle metadata, dividers, inactive states |
| Washed Periwinkle | `#ADB4C8` | Soft highlight, hover states, quiet accents |

### Recommended Ratios

- 70% Warm Bone / off-white
- 20% Deep Navy / Almost Black Navy
- 10% gray-blue accents

For posters or release graphics, invert the ratio:

- 70% Deep Navy
- 20% Warm Bone
- 10% gray-blue accents

### Color Rules

- Prefer two-color compositions.
- Use warm bone instead of pure white.
- Use deep navy instead of pure black when possible.
- Keep accent colors restrained and slightly faded.
- Avoid neon unless it appears in a specific show flyer or album campaign.

## Typography

No exact font could be identified from the public Instagram metadata. Use fonts that match the observed handmade, DIY, emotionally noisy identity.

### Primary Web Typeface

Use a plain system sans:

```css
font-family: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif;
```

Why:

- Unfussy
- DIY web feeling
- Easy to read
- Close to personal artist-site language

### Display / Poster Type

Use sparingly:

- A condensed grotesk
- A typewriter face
- A rough photocopy/scan treatment
- Hand-lettered text provided by the band
- Set-aesthetic / staged-environment styling, because Drive notes say Magnus likes “SET AESTHETICS”

Recommended free/Google-font directions if adding fonts later:

- `Space Grotesk` for clearer modern layouts
- `IBM Plex Mono` for tracklists, captions, metadata
- `Special Elite` only for occasional rough typewriter moments

Do not overuse novelty fonts. The brand should feel handmade, not costume-y.

## Layout & Web Styling

The website should stay simple and blog-like.

Use:

- Narrow content column
- Stacked sections
- Plain links
- Small headings
- Minimal cards
- Lots of text that feels updateable
- Simple image grids
- Small captions
- Unvarnished metadata like dates, set times, locations
- Behind-the-scenes content areas for writing, recording, studio, and show prep

Avoid:

- Hero sections that feel like a startup landing page
- Big gradient backgrounds
- Rounded glossy cards
- Marketing copy
- Heavy animation
- Polished product UI

Preferred CSS feel:

- Warm off-white background
- Deep navy text or accents
- Thin gray-blue borders
- Underlined text links
- Slightly uneven/DIY image treatment where appropriate

## Photography & Media Direction

Use media that feels documentary and close to the band.

Good subjects:

- Practice rooms
- Cables, pedals, amps, paper setlists
- Dim venue corners
- Show flyers
- Blurry live shots
- Scanned lyric sheets
- Recording process
- Friends and small rooms
- Weathered textures: pavement, water, tape, paper, walls
- Berkeley show spaces and campus-adjacent rooms
- KALX / studio BTS
- “Public School Records” context when relevant
- Setlists and covers that reveal taste

Treatment:

- Natural grain is good.
- Flash photos are good.
- Slight blur is acceptable.
- Keep color grading muted.
- Crop loosely.
- Captions can be casual and specific.

Avoid:

- Stock photos
- Overly polished press-kit images
- Generic ocean imagery
- Literal whale imagery unless tied to actual band art
- Bright influencer-style edits

## Content Strategy

Drive materials point toward an authentic, process-heavy rollout rather than polished ads.

Good content pillars:

- **BTS songwriting / recording:** process clips, room audio, false starts, pedals, lyrics, tracking, band discussion.
- **Live performance intensity:** compilations of shows, loud transitions, builds, drops, crowd/room moments.
- **Song-focused clips:** use timestamps from content notes to frame short videos.
- **Local scene context:** Berkeley, Public School Records, Pike, Gilman, KALX, small rooms, campus-adjacent shows.
- **Flyers and handouts:** Drive notes say Whalefall flyers were liked and passed around; keep flyer energy rough and memorable.
- **Merch ideas:** stickers and shirts are explicitly mentioned in Drive notes.

Song/content notes from Drive:

- `Starboy`: intro/instrumental can support fun band-clip combinations.
- `For You, to Blame`: intro can support recording/writing BTS; build/transition can support intense performance compilation; calm-then-pickup section can support lyric video or stress/narrative concept; ending can support recap/thank-you style content.

Rollout principles:

- Keep the approach authentic.
- Show process instead of pretending the band appeared fully formed.
- Let videos feel close, loud, and occasionally messy.
- Use short interviews and casual questions when introducing the band.
- Favor “in the room with them” over “official campaign.”

## Voice & Copy

Tone:

- Plainspoken
- Short
- Personal
- Slightly poetic but not precious
- Emotionally direct
- Comfortable with lowercase
- Comfortable with fragments

Good copy patterns:

- `emotional noise from berkeley, ca`
- `new songs from the room with the bad light`
- `photos from the show`
- `practice notes`
- `lyrics / scraps`
- `doors at 7, we play at 8`
- `newest songs`
- `behind the noise`
- `recording notes`
- `berkeley rock out`
- `see you next semester`

Avoid:

- `Experience the sound of...`
- `An immersive musical journey...`
- `We are excited to announce...`
- Corporate press-release language

## UI Components

### Links

- Use underlined text links.
- Keep platform links simple: Bandcamp, Spotify, Apple Music, SoundCloud, YouTube.
- Avoid big pill buttons unless the design specifically calls for a poster-like treatment.

### Sections

Section names should be plain:

- New Release
- Listen
- Recent Shows
- Studio / Media Progress
- Recent Blog Posts
- Sketches / Lyrics
- Guestbook
- Contact

### Images

- Add thin borders in `#51576B` or `#979CAA`.
- Use simple captions.
- Prefer actual band media over decorative imagery.

## Practical Rules For Future Work

When creating anything for Whalefall:

1. Start with Warm Bone and Deep Navy.
2. Keep the layout simple and updateable.
3. Use plain language and avoid marketing polish.
4. Include actual band context: places, dates, shows, notes, rooms, process.
5. Make media feel found, scanned, taped-up, or documented.
6. Use the phrase `emotional noise from berkeley, ca` as the voice anchor.
7. Prefer under-designed honesty over polished decoration.
8. Use Drive-backed music positioning: alt-rock, garage rock, indie, experimental rock, shoegaze.
9. Let social/content ideas lean into BTS, performance intensity, show prep, and campus/local scene texture.
10. Keep merch/flyer directions flexible around stickers, shirts, set aesthetics, and possible whale-with-X-eyes motifs.
